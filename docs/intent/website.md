# Website behavior and maintenance

The site showcases Guille Ojeda’s writing, talks, books, and community work. It is a static Astro site, generated in English at `/` and Spanish at `/es/` from a shared layout and small bilingual content modules.

LinkedIn is the contact destination, including speaking invitations. Simple AWS sponsorship inquiries go to Passionfroot. The site does not offer consulting. Publication titles can retain their original language; navigation, descriptions, metadata, and accessibility text are localized.

Content lives in the repository. A work item stores common identifiers and destinations once, with its English and Spanish descriptions together. Updating a role or adding a talk should not require editing page templates. The site links to every supported publication hub rather than importing individual articles or refreshing feeds at runtime. Featured work cards are a selection, with the remaining writing hubs, white papers, resources, and projects available in a native disclosure. Historical experiments and prototypes carry explicit labels. Featured speaking cards are a selection; the complete year-grouped archive includes supported talks, workshops, hosted events, podcasts, training sessions, and video appearances. Native disclosure keeps that archive usable without JavaScript. Records without a public destination remain readable entries. Hosted events identify the host role explicitly; guest talks are not attributed to Guille. Ongoing organizing roles remain under community activities. Keynote reaction videos are commentary, not conference sessions. White papers identify authorship separately from publisher affiliation. Guille owns the content and both translations.

Historical book credits do not imply current availability. The third book is explicitly in progress; the historical video course is marked no longer available. These statuses are separate from published book credits. Community Builder and User Group Leader are distinct roles; the latter names AWS AI User Group Argentina.

## Content statistics

The Content & reach section sits between Selected work and Talks. Separate Articles, Talks & sessions, and Books cards report cumulative counts from the covered sources or maintained inventory. A fourth Newsletter card reports Simple AWS’s current active subscriptions from Beehiiv, independently of cumulative publication counts or an activity period. Articles may also show independently sourced Simple AWS Views and Google Search clicks. Search clicks are reported Search Console events, distinct from Views and not unique readers; filtering may make the covered result partial. Each measure carries its own unit, source coverage, interval, timezone and last successful collection date. These are covered-inventory figures, not complete career totals, readership or sales measures. Unsupported figures are not shown as zero. Cards use two columns where space permits and one on mobile.

The optional Simple AWS article source covers its currently active published Beehiiv catalog across web-only, email-only and combined posts. Drafts, future scheduled posts and archived posts are excluded. The catalog does not establish deleted or archived history, successful email delivery, or a complete record of writing.

Article equality uses **normalized title + actual content language**, uniformly within and across every article source, including future integrations. Unicode NFC, lowercase and collapsed whitespace remove spelling-format differences; punctuation and accents remain significant. Matching titles in the same language count once, while different titles and separately published languages count separately. Renamed copies therefore count separately under the owner's chosen rule. Provider IDs, publication URLs, original-only source policies and canonical/origin links do not override it. Keep all eligible publication URLs separate from count deduplication: supported views include originals, copies and translations independently of count identities.

The optional Simple AWS source is an exact `{ "id": "simple-aws" }` selector that reuses the existing English newsletter publication ID and language. Its article scope includes that resolved publication and language; enabling or removing the source or changing its publication changes article scope. Source order, API-key location/value and observed inventory do not. Newsletter, Views and Search keep their independent scopes.

The shared All · English · Español selector filters content language independently of the interface language. Caylent uses the owner's confirmed English policy; Builder Center uses each published article's stored locale. For maintained talk and book facts, always classify language from the original content title, as the owner requested; translated interface descriptions do not change it. English product names in a Spanish title do not make it English. Store the classification explicitly, without a runtime language detector. Unknown/unsupported languages are excluded. A successful empty eligible inventory displays zero; missing or invalid data remains unavailable. Simple AWS is owner-confirmed English: All and English show the same subscription, Views and Search scope; Español keeps its article count while unsupported activity measures stay unavailable and the newsletter card stays hidden. A missing/invalid newsletter group remains unavailable because its applicable language is not established. The native Sources and counting disclosure explains the rules and partial coverage and links to the statistics privacy page. That short bilingual page describes the owner's private Google-authorized tool, aggregate Analytics and Search Console use and publication, latest-total retention and revocation. Visitors do not connect Google accounts.

Speaking counts confirmed delivered talks, workshops, training, panels and technical sessions. Repeated deliveries count separately even with matching titles; speaker participation still counts when Guille also hosted. Host-only appearances, commentary, podcasts/conversations and scheduled/unknown deliveries are excluded. Each maintained occurrence has its own identity, kind, status and language; grouped records can contain separate slots that complete independently. Books count published records, including unavailable historical credits; in-progress and unknown publication states are excluded. Both published books are English. Their source label is Website inventory, localized in the Spanish interface.

One daily EventBridge Scheduler invocation at 09:15 America/Argentina/Cordoba runs a Node 24 Lambda. It reads current protected inputs and derives independent article, speaking, book, newsletter, article-view and Search Console click measures. Article collection exhausts the Caylent and Builder Center author-filtered lists and, when selected, every page of the Simple AWS Beehiiv catalog. It counts confirmed posts with valid titles, platforms and publication dates no later than collection time across all platforms; drafts, future schedules and archived posts are excluded. Missing eligible metadata, failed pages, malformed responses or incomplete pagination fail the article group. Caylent's editorial date and optional publication schedule bounds remain distinct from CMS timestamps. Builder Center's list supplies the count metadata without article-body requests. Builder Center uses an observed first-party interface; interface changes may require an adapter correction before that group refreshes.

Valid groups can refresh while another group fails. When publishing fresh siblings, the collector reads the prior aggregate once and retains any valid, supported failed group with its exact original count, source, date, interval and scope. Search retention preserves its exact source, Pacific interval, scope and collection date. A Search report failure does not suppress Views, counts or subscribers; a shared Google credential failure affects both Google families. An absent or unsupported prior group is omitted rather than replaced by zero. Missing S3 objects are absence; other storage errors, malformed JSON/root schemas and failed writes fail the invocation without replacement. If no group succeeds freshly, the collector fails without writing. One complete validated object publishes the available/retained groups. Newsletter retention preserves its exact publication identity and language even after a failed configuration change. No article-level performance, subscriber records, private item lists, raw provider responses or daily history are stored.

The collector uses shared regional Lambda concurrency. Every invocation recomputes the full current snapshot, so if executions overlap, the one that finishes last can publish an older complete snapshot.

Normal builds run `scripts/build-content-stats-inputs.mjs` after Astro, combining maintained schema 2 article configuration with speaking/book facts. Caylent and Builder Center remain required article sources; the optional exact Simple AWS source entry reuses the existing English newsletter selector and is available with input4, 5 or 6. Generated protected inputs still use 3 without newsletter, 4 with newsletter, 5 with newsletter plus analytics, and 6 only with the exact Search Console selector alongside both existing input5 selections. Adding Simple AWS does not change these versions. The production Search selection uses the verified Simple AWS English article scope and `availableSince: 2025-06-19`, an available-history boundary rather than a lifetime or continuous-tracking claim. Curated inputs contain only identities/kind/status/language; no duplicated display copy or computed constants. Analytics selection is a strict known publication/property/host/path/language/timezone scope, with a real optional source-backed recorded start. Search selection is a strict known Domain property, English language, canonical article regex and real `availableSince` boundary. Unknown fields, private fields or malformed selectors fail generation and collection before provider work. Runtime compatibility retains inputs 1–6; the title/language count rule stays uniform. Publish compatible runtime and client code while the existing two-source selection remains active, then enable Simple AWS through normal CI. To roll back, remove the Simple AWS selection through CI and invoke the compatible collector to publish actual remaining coverage before restoring any client/runtime that cannot accept three-source output. Inputs never contain private credentials. CloudFront is explicitly denied access to that prefix. The collector alone writes `/content-stats/v1/current.json`; its S3 permission reads only the exact input and prior aggregate and writes only the exact aggregate. Site CI is denied writes there and rejects artifacts containing that prefix. The public object remains schema1. Existing count/current groups retain their contracts; independent `articleViews` and `articleSearchClicks` contain only safe period aggregates and provenance, rendered inside Articles rather than as extra cards. Newsletter uses a current scalar count with explicit publication identity/language; its scope includes the provider selector and language but excludes the credential location/value and observed total. Mutable JSON revalidates through the existing cache policy, without a daily distribution invalidation.

Beehiiv’s API key lives in an operator-owned Secrets Manager secret whose JSON SecretString contains `apiKey`. CloudFormation receives its full ARN, passes it as `BEEHIIV_API_KEY_SECRET_ARN`, and grants only exact-secret `GetSecretValue`; neither the secret resource nor value is managed by the stack or site CI. Input4/5 reads the current value once per invocation; legacy inputs do not read it. The collector requests only the configured publication’s active-subscription aggregate from Beehiiv’s fixed HTTPS origin, refuses redirects, and applies the existing ten-second deadline and one transient retry. Only a safe nonnegative integer is measured data; missing or boolean statistics cannot become zero. The displayed date is successful fetch time because Beehiiv supplies no statistic-computation timestamp. No raw provider response, credential or provider publication ID enters public output or logs.

Article views use only Simple AWS's configured English article hostname/full-path scope, independently of the Caylent/Builder count inventory. Its verified source timezone is `America/Cordoba`; no continuous tracking start is established, so recorded total remains unavailable. The provider query is metric-only `screenPageViews` with exact hostname AND full-regexp article path; it includes all matching older/original/republished/translated pages, excludes unrelated landing/archive/subscribe/poll pages and returns no page-level rows. Search clicks use only the exact Simple AWS Search Console Domain property and anchored canonical article URL regex, with Web search type, final data and aggregate response. Search reports use America/Los_Angeles calendar dates, end three days before collection, and do not claim lifetime history; a full period beginning before `availableSince` is unavailable. Browser/device language never determines content language. No GA4/Search/Beehiiv double count or sum of unique people is created.

The shared native period selector offers 7/30/90 days, calendar 12 months (default), year to date and an optional Views recorded total; Search has no total or lifetime period. Only activity measures follow it; counts stay cumulative and subscribers current. Compute dates in each source timezone: Views in the verified Analytics timezone and Search in `America/Los_Angeles`, always ending three closed calendar days before collection; show each record's actual interval, timezone and last successful fetch. Search keeps a full requested window only when its start is supported by `availableSince`; it is omitted rather than shortened. YTD uses the collection year's January 1 and stays unavailable while the reporting delay lies in the previous year. Optional recorded totals say Views since the verified date; absent evidence means no total, not invented lifetime. A configured later history start makes an earlier requested window unsupported rather than shortening it. Figures are reported data subject to revision.

Google access uses an owner-authorized offline OAuth credential in one operator-owned standard SSM SecureString. The stack takes only its full ARN, passes `GOOGLE_OAUTH_PARAMETER_ARN` and grants exact `GetParameter`; no broad list/decrypt policy or credential resource is added. One decrypt and one refresh per enabled invocation supply one in-memory token shared by Analytics and Search Console through fixed HTTPS token/report endpoints, refusing redirects and preserving the ten-second deadline/one transient retry. Input5 uses Analytics read-only access; input6 requires exactly Analytics readonly plus Search Console `webmasters.readonly`, with no write scopes. The initial private app's supported production/personal-use setup avoids Testing's seven-day refresh expiry. No custom login service is maintained.

Each Views period validates its numeric aggregate/header/shape/timezone independently. Search validates one aggregate row, nonnegative safe-integer clicks, final-data metadata and its exact Pacific interval; explicit zero is data, while empty, missing, grouped, incomplete or malformed responses are unavailable. Sampling, thresholding or other-row metadata qualifies reported values; date-truncated intervals cannot replace a complete requested window. A failed period can retain its exact previous value/source/scope/interval/qualifications/fetch date while siblings refresh. Unsupported combinations are omitted; prior-year YTD is never relabeled current. Removing analytics intentionally removes the views family without reading Google; removing Search intentionally removes its family. Public JSON/logs contain no raw responses, private credentials, account/property/stream IDs, URL/page/query rows or per-article performance.

The browser fetches once and applies both native selectors locally. ## Rendering and routing

Ordinary content and language links work without JavaScript; statistics show a localized enable-JavaScript message. English and Spanish are separate HTML documents. Interface language selection is explicit and opens the corresponding page in the other language; it is not inferred from browser settings or persisted in cookies. The statistics privacy pages are `/content-statistics/privacy/` and `/es/content-statistics/privacy/`, with their own canonical/alternate metadata and ordinary navigation back to the home-page sections.

The public origin comes from the build’s `SITE_URL`, which is `https://guilleojeda.com` in production. Both locales use that value consistently for canonical, alternate-language, and social metadata. It must describe the deployed endpoint.

CloudFront reads a private S3 bucket through Origin Access Control. A small viewer-request function first redirects only the exact `www.guilleojeda.com` host to `https://guilleojeda.com`, retaining the complete URI and raw query string. The apex and CloudFront hostnames continue through the normal routing: the two home and two statistics privacy routes serve their exact directory `index.html` objects. Known routes without a trailing slash redirect to it while retaining the query. Assets and other paths pass through; unknown paths return HTTP 404 with links to both home pages rather than a successful homepage response.

The custom certificate covers the apex and `www` names and is DNS validated through the Route 53 hosted zone. The apex A and AAAA records alias the existing CloudFront distribution; `www` uses the same aliases so the function can issue the canonical redirect. Registration remains at GoDaddy. The Route 53 zone preserves the blog, mail, verification, and other non-provider records copied during the launch.

## Delivery and recovery

Pull-request builds have no AWS deployment privileges and pass the resulting artifact to the main-branch deployment job. That job uses GitHub OIDC and a resource-scoped AWS role. Repository variables contain public identifiers and the deployment URL, not long-lived AWS credentials. Change-set creation is restricted by name and template URL; inspection and execution are scoped to the existing stack. Lambda actions target only the collector; `iam:GetRole` reads are limited to the existing collector execution-role and scheduler-role ARNs and the same-stack `SiteDeploymentRole-*` prefix used to resolve `DeploymentRoleArn` without a self-reference; the `HostedZoneNameServers` output read handler uses only `route53:GetHostedZone`, `route53:ListTagsForResource`, and `route53:ListQueryLoggingConfigs` on `SiteHostedZone`; the `DistributionDomainName` read handler uses only `cloudfront:GetDistribution`, `cloudfront:GetDistributionConfig`, and `cloudfront:ListTagsForResource` on `SiteDistribution`; these are read-only handler permissions and add no DNS or distribution configuration/tag writes; role passing is limited to the collector execution role and Lambda service. The deploy script enforces the code-only template/change-set rule; IAM scopes the resources but does not encode the nested `Code.ZipFile` comparison. The script rejects a stack service role so the existing caller permissions remain the effective boundary. CI cannot invoke the collector, write the public aggregate, or read collector inputs and credentials.

Production publication is serialized. A superseded main-branch push must not replace a newer build. Hashed assets are uploaded before mutable HTML and metadata; old hashed assets are retained so previously cached pages remain usable. An age-only deletion rule could remove a current asset that has not changed, so none is used. This accepts small storage growth in exchange for simple, reliable cached-page and rollback behavior; reconsider cleanup only if measured growth warrants it.

After a one-time operator update of the CI policy, collector code-only changes deploy through the existing CloudFormation stack at the start of the serialized main-branch site job. The renderer embeds the authoritative collector source, just as it embeds the routing function. CI compares the deployed original template with the rendered template and permits only `ContentStatsCollector.Properties.Code.ZipFile` to change. Its change set must include one in-place update to that Lambda. CloudFormation may also preview `ContentStatsSchedulerRole`, `ContentStatsDailySchedule` and `SiteDeploymentRole`. The script accepts at most one `ContentStatsSchedulerRole` preview only when every detail is a dynamic `Properties.Policies` reference caused by `ContentStatsCollector.Arn`; one `ContentStatsDailySchedule` preview only when every detail is a dynamic `Properties.Target` reference caused by either `ContentStatsCollector.Arn` or `ContentStatsSchedulerRole.Arn`; and one `SiteDeploymentRole` preview only when every detail is a dynamic `Properties.Policies` reference caused by `ContentStatsSchedulerRole.Arn`. Direct, static, replacement, duplicate or unknown changes fail before execution. It preserves existing stack parameters and waits for completion. It keeps the Lambda signed code URL in memory, fetches the archive without logging the URL or source, and uses a temporary archive with the system `unzip` command to verify the deployed `index.js` bytes and hash. The scoped Lambda permissions cover the CloudFormation handler's code/configuration, tag and metadata operations. `iam:GetRole` reads are limited to the two existing role ARNs and the same-stack `SiteDeploymentRole-*` prefix needed to read the `DeploymentRoleArn` output without a self-reference; the full Route53 read handler (`GetHostedZone`, `ListTagsForResource`, `ListQueryLoggingConfigs`) reads only `SiteHostedZone` for `HostedZoneNameServers`, and the full CloudFront read handler (`GetDistribution`, `GetDistributionConfig`, `ListTagsForResource`) reads only `SiteDistribution` for `DistributionDomainName`; these additions grant no DNS or distribution configuration/tag writes; pass-role access targets only the collector execution role for Lambda; the initial live CI update is the account-specific check of this permission set. Setup and every other infrastructure change remain operator-managed. Ordinary configuration edits use site CI and are read at the next collection; they do not require a Lambda deployment. Bounded request/pagination and invocation retries cannot publish a partial inventory.

HTML and mutable metadata revalidate; hashed assets can be cached immutably. CloudFront invalidation and direct live checks are part of deployment. Uploading several objects is not atomic: a failure can leave mixed files until the same intended revision is redeployed. A failed build publishes nothing, and a failed deployment is reported as failed rather than hidden by a successful build.

Rollback is a reviewed Git revert on `main`, delivered through the same workflow. AWS configuration is versioned in CloudFormation, with setup and infrastructure changes performed through a separately authorized operator session. Before changing DNS authority, compare the full unrelated-record inventory and reconcile DNSSEC/parent DS state. DNS rollback requires a verified complete zone at the previous provider before restoring its delegation. The Route 53 hosted zone is retained if removed from the stack. Account-wide identity resources are reused when present rather than replaced or deleted as a side effect of this site.

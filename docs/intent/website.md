# Website behavior and maintenance

The site showcases Guille Ojeda’s writing, talks, books, and community work. It is a static Astro site, generated in English at `/` and Spanish at `/es/` from a shared layout and small bilingual content modules.

LinkedIn is the contact destination, including speaking invitations. Simple AWS sponsorship inquiries go to Passionfroot. The site does not offer consulting. Publication titles can retain their original language; navigation, descriptions, metadata, and accessibility text are localized.

Content lives in the repository. A work item stores common identifiers and destinations once, with its English and Spanish descriptions together. Updating a role or adding a talk should not require editing page templates. The site links to every supported publication hub rather than importing individual articles or refreshing feeds at runtime. Featured work cards are a selection, with the remaining writing hubs, white papers, resources, and projects available in a native disclosure. Historical experiments and prototypes carry explicit labels. Featured speaking cards are a selection; the complete year-grouped archive includes supported talks, workshops, hosted events, podcasts, training sessions, and video appearances. Native disclosure keeps that archive usable without JavaScript. Records without a public destination remain readable entries. Hosted events identify the host role explicitly; guest talks are not attributed to Guille. Ongoing organizing roles remain under community activities. Keynote reaction videos are commentary, not conference sessions. White papers identify authorship separately from publisher affiliation. Guille owns the content and both translations.

Historical book credits do not imply current availability. The third book is explicitly in progress; the historical video course is marked no longer available. These statuses are separate from published book credits. Community Builder and User Group Leader are distinct roles; the latter names AWS AI User Group Argentina.

## Content statistics

The Content & reach section sits between Selected work and Talks. Separate Articles, Talks & sessions, and Books cards report cumulative counts from the covered sources or maintained inventory. A fourth Newsletter card reports Simple AWS’s current active subscriptions from Beehiiv, independently of cumulative publication counts or an activity period. Each card carries its own source coverage and last successful collection date. These are covered-inventory figures, not complete career totals, readership or sales measures. Views and other unsupported figures are not shown as zero. Cards use two columns where space permits and one on mobile.

Article equality uses **normalized title + actual content language**, uniformly within and across every article source, including future integrations. Unicode NFC, lowercase and collapsed whitespace remove spelling-format differences; punctuation and accents remain significant. Matching titles in the same language count once, while different titles and separately published languages count separately. Renamed copies therefore count separately under the owner's chosen rule. Provider IDs, publication URLs, original-only source policies and canonical/origin links do not override it. Keep all eligible publication URLs separate from count deduplication: supported views include originals, copies and translations independently of count identities.

The shared All · English · Español selector filters content language independently of the interface language. Caylent uses the owner's confirmed English policy; Builder Center uses each published article's stored locale. For maintained talk and book facts, always classify language from the original content title, as the owner requested; translated interface descriptions do not change it. English product names in a Spanish title do not make it English. Store the classification explicitly, without a runtime language detector. Unknown/unsupported languages are excluded. A successful empty eligible inventory displays zero; missing or invalid data remains unavailable. Simple AWS is owner-confirmed English: All and English show the same subscription total; Español hides the valid newsletter card rather than displaying a false zero. A missing/invalid newsletter group remains unavailable because its applicable language is not established. The native Sources and counting disclosure explains the rules and partial coverage and links to the statistics privacy page. That short bilingual page describes the owner's private Google-authorized tool, aggregate data use and publication, encrypted credentials, latest-total retention and revocation. Visitors do not connect Google accounts.

Speaking counts confirmed delivered talks, workshops, training, panels and technical sessions. Repeated deliveries count separately even with matching titles; speaker participation still counts when Guille also hosted. Host-only appearances, commentary, podcasts/conversations and scheduled/unknown deliveries are excluded. Each maintained occurrence has its own identity, kind, status and language; grouped records can contain separate slots that complete independently. Books count published records, including unavailable historical credits; in-progress and unknown publication states are excluded. Both published books are English. Their source label is Website inventory, localized in the Spanish interface.

One daily EventBridge Scheduler invocation at 09:15 America/Argentina/Cordoba runs a Node 24 Lambda. It reads current protected inputs and derives independent article, speaking, book, newsletter and configured article-view measures. Article collection exhausts both author-filtered source lists and checks authorship, publication state/date and required metadata. Caylent's editorial date and optional publication schedule bounds remain distinct from CMS timestamps. Builder Center's list supplies the count metadata without article-body requests. Missing eligible titles, failed pages, malformed responses or pagination-cap exhaustion fail the article group. Builder Center uses an observed first-party interface; interface changes may require an adapter correction before that group refreshes.

Valid groups can refresh while another group fails. When publishing fresh siblings, the collector reads the prior aggregate once and retains any valid, supported failed group with its exact original count, source, date and scope. An absent or unsupported prior group is omitted rather than replaced by zero. Missing S3 objects are absence; other storage errors, malformed JSON/root schemas and failed writes fail the invocation without replacement. If no group succeeds freshly, the collector fails without writing. One complete validated object publishes the available/retained groups. Newsletter retention preserves its exact publication identity and language even after a failed configuration change. No article-level performance, subscriber records, private item lists, raw provider responses or daily history are stored.

The collector uses shared regional Lambda concurrency. Every invocation recomputes the full current snapshot, so if executions overlap, the one that finishes last can publish an older complete snapshot.

Normal builds run `scripts/build-content-stats-inputs.mjs` after Astro, combining maintained schema 2 article configuration with speaking/book facts. Generated protected inputs use 3 without newsletter,4 with newsletter,5 with newsletter plus analytics. Curated inputs contain only identities/kind/status/language; no duplicated display copy or computed constants. Analytics selection is a strict known publication/property/host/path/language/timezone scope, with a real optional source-backed recorded start. Unknown fields or malformed selectors fail generation and collection before provider work. Runtime compatibility retains inputs 1–4; the title/language count rule stays uniform. Publish a compatible runtime/IAM first, then a compatible client with inputs 4, then enable 5 through normal CI. Roll back to 4 and invoke the compatible collector before restoring a client that cannot accept the views family.

Inputs never contain private credentials. CloudFront is explicitly denied access to that prefix. The collector alone writes `/content-stats/v1/current.json`; its S3 permission reads only the exact input and prior aggregate and writes only the exact aggregate. Site CI is denied writes there and rejects artifacts containing that prefix. The public object remains schema1. Existing count/current groups retain their contracts; independent `articleViews` contains only safe period aggregates and provenance, rendered inside Articles rather than as a fifth card. Newsletter uses a current scalar count with explicit publication identity/language; its scope includes the provider selector and language but excludes the credential location/value and observed total. Mutable JSON revalidates through the existing cache policy, without a daily distribution invalidation.

Beehiiv’s API key lives in an operator-owned Secrets Manager secret whose JSON SecretString contains `apiKey`. CloudFormation receives its full ARN, passes it as `BEEHIIV_API_KEY_SECRET_ARN`, and grants only exact-secret `GetSecretValue`; neither the secret resource nor value is managed by the stack or site CI. Input4/5 reads the current value once per invocation; legacy inputs do not read it. The collector requests only the configured publication’s active-subscription aggregate from Beehiiv’s fixed HTTPS origin, refuses redirects, and applies the existing ten-second deadline and one transient retry. Only a safe nonnegative integer is measured data; missing or boolean statistics cannot become zero. The displayed date is successful fetch time because Beehiiv supplies no statistic-computation timestamp. No raw provider response, credential or provider publication ID enters public output or logs.

Article views use only Simple AWS's configured English article hostname/full-path scope, independently of the Caylent/Builder count inventory. Its verified source timezone is `America/Cordoba`; no continuous tracking start is established, so recorded total remains unavailable. The provider query is metric-only `screenPageViews` with exact hostname AND full-regexp article path; it includes all matching older/original/republished/translated pages, excludes unrelated landing/archive/subscribe/poll pages and returns no page-level rows. Browser/device language never determines content language. No GA4/Beehiiv double count or sum of unique people is created.

The shared native period selector offers 7/30/90 days, calendar 12 months (default), year to date and optional recorded total. Only activity measures follow it; counts stay cumulative and subscribers current. Compute dates in the source timezone, ending three closed calendar days before collection; show each record's actual interval, timezone and last successful fetch. YTD uses the collection year's January 1 and stays unavailable while the reporting delay lies in the previous year. Optional recorded totals say Views since the verified date; absent evidence means no total, not invented lifetime. A configured later history start makes an earlier requested window unsupported rather than shortening it. Figures are reported data subject to revision.

Google access uses an owner-authorized offline OAuth credential in one operator-owned standard SSM SecureString. The stack takes only its full ARN, passes `GOOGLE_OAUTH_PARAMETER_ARN` and grants exact `GetParameter`; no broad list/decrypt policy or credential resource is added. One decrypted read/refresh per enabled invocation supplies in-memory access for fixed HTTPS token/report endpoints, refusing redirects and preserving the ten-second deadline/one transient retry. Only Analytics read scope is requested. The initial private app's supported production/personal-use setup avoids Testing's seven-day refresh expiry. No custom login service is maintained.

Each views period validates its numeric aggregate/header/shape/timezone independently. An explicit safe integer0 is data; missing/malformed reports are unavailable. Sampling, thresholding or other-row metadata qualifies reported values; date-truncated intervals cannot replace a complete requested window. A failed period can retain its exact previous value/source/scope/interval/qualifications/fetch date while siblings refresh. Unsupported combinations are omitted; prior-year YTD is never relabeled current. Removing analytics intentionally removes the views family without reading Google. Public JSON/logs contain no raw responses, private credentials, account/property/stream IDs or per-article performance.

The browser fetches once and applies both native selectors locally. Views and article counts validate/render independently, with localized units/dates/qualifications and cleared stale fields when a combination is unavailable. All/English shows Simple AWS views; Español keeps its count with unavailable views. Recorded total is disabled without an applicable supported record. It accepts saved article-only snapshots, retaining their actual coverage/date while new cards remain unavailable. Missing or invalid known groups affect only their own cards; unsupported root schemas or unknown group keys make all statistics unavailable. Supported measured zeros render normally. Statistics require JavaScript; ordinary site content and navigation remain usable without it.

## Rendering and routing

Ordinary content and language links work without JavaScript; statistics show a localized enable-JavaScript message. English and Spanish are separate HTML documents. Interface language selection is explicit and opens the corresponding page in the other language; it is not inferred from browser settings or persisted in cookies. The statistics privacy pages are `/content-statistics/privacy/` and `/es/content-statistics/privacy/`, with their own canonical/alternate metadata and ordinary navigation back to the home-page sections.

The public origin comes from the build’s `SITE_URL`, which is `https://guilleojeda.com` in production. Both locales use that value consistently for canonical, alternate-language, and social metadata. It must describe the deployed endpoint.

CloudFront reads a private S3 bucket through Origin Access Control. A small viewer-request function first redirects only the exact `www.guilleojeda.com` host to `https://guilleojeda.com`, retaining the complete URI and raw query string. The apex and CloudFront hostnames continue through the normal routing: the two home and two statistics privacy routes serve their exact directory `index.html` objects. Known routes without a trailing slash redirect to it while retaining the query. Assets and other paths pass through; unknown paths return HTTP 404 with links to both home pages rather than a successful homepage response.

The custom certificate covers the apex and `www` names and is DNS validated through the Route 53 hosted zone. The apex A and AAAA records alias the existing CloudFront distribution; `www` uses the same aliases so the function can issue the canonical redirect. Registration remains at GoDaddy. The Route 53 zone preserves the blog, mail, verification, and other non-provider records copied during the launch.

## Delivery and recovery

GitHub Actions builds without AWS deployment privileges and passes the resulting artifact to the main-branch deployment job. That job uses GitHub OIDC and a resource-scoped AWS role. Repository variables contain public identifiers and the deployment URL, not long-lived AWS credentials. The content-deployment role cannot administer infrastructure.

Production publication is serialized. A superseded main-branch push must not replace a newer build. Hashed assets are uploaded before mutable HTML and metadata; old hashed assets are retained so previously cached pages remain usable. An age-only deletion rule could remove a current asset that has not changed, so none is used. This accepts small storage growth in exchange for simple, reliable cached-page and rollback behavior; reconsider cleanup only if measured growth warrants it.

Collector code and AWS resources are deployed through the existing operator CloudFormation path. The infrastructure renderer embeds the authoritative collector source, just as it embeds the routing function. Ordinary configuration edits use site CI and are read at the next collection; they do not require a Lambda deployment. Bounded request/pagination and invocation retries cannot publish a partial inventory.

HTML and mutable metadata revalidate; hashed assets can be cached immutably. CloudFront invalidation and direct live checks are part of deployment. Uploading several objects is not atomic: a failure can leave mixed files until the same intended revision is redeployed. A failed build publishes nothing, and a failed deployment is reported as failed rather than hidden by a successful build.

Rollback is a reviewed Git revert on `main`, delivered through the same workflow. AWS configuration is versioned in CloudFormation, with setup and infrastructure changes performed through a separately authorized operator session. Before changing DNS authority, compare the full unrelated-record inventory and reconcile DNSSEC/parent DS state. DNS rollback requires a verified complete zone at the previous provider before restoring its delegation. The Route 53 hosted zone is retained if removed from the stack. Account-wide identity resources are reused when present rather than replaced or deleted as a side effect of this site.
